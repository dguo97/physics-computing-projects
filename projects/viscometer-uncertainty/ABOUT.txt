VISCOMETER UNCERTAINTY
======================

WHAT IT DOES
Shows how uncertain a viscosity measurement is when it comes from the falling-sphere method: drop a
ball into a thick liquid, time how long it takes to fall a set distance, and work out how "thick"
(viscous) the liquid is.

HOW IT WORKS
Every measurement (ball size, ball mass, liquid mass and volume, fall distance, fall time) has a
small uncertainty. The program repeats the experiment 10,000 times on the computer, each time picking
every measurement at random within its uncertainty, and calculates the viscosity every time. The
spread of the 10,000 answers is the uncertainty of the result.

OUTPUT
- Viscosity about 35.0 +/- 0.8 Pa s, printed.
- output/viscosity_distribution.png - a histogram of the 10,000 answers.
- output/convergence.png (from viscometer_convergence.py) - how the average improves with more runs.
The ball diameter, ball mass and fall time contribute most of the uncertainty.

UNITS NOTE
The liquid volume 0.1 is treated as cubic metres, as in the original code. If it should be litres,
use --liquid-volume-in-litres (the answer becomes about 25.2 Pa s).

RUN IT
python viscometer.py --n 50000 --seed 1
python viscometer_convergence.py
