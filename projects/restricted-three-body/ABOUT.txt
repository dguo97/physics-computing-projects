RESTRICTED THREE-BODY PROBLEM
=============================

WHAT IT DOES
Follows a tiny satellite as it moves under the gravity of two big bodies that circle each other
(think of a spacecraft in the Earth-Moon system; the mass ratio used is 0.01228). The motion is
viewed from a frame that turns along with the two big bodies.

WHY IT IS INTERESTING
Three bodies have no exact formula for their motion, so it must be simulated step by step. The
starting conditions here are special: they give a periodic orbit (the Arenstorf orbit) that repeats
itself exactly every 17.065 time units.

HOW IT WORKS
The program uses fourth-order Runge-Kutta (RK4) with 100,000 steps. The orbit passes close to one
of the big bodies, which is why so many steps are needed.

OUTPUT
- output/three_body_trajectory.png - the looping path of the satellite.
- output/three_body_coordinates.png and output/three_body_velocity.png
- output/convergence.png (from three_body_convergence.py) - accuracy study.

CHECKS
After one period the satellite is back where it started, and a conserved quantity (the Jacobi
constant) stays constant to about one part in a million.

RUN IT
python three_body.py
python three_body_convergence.py
